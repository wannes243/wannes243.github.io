Autonomous Delivery Fleet

My team’s entry in the TSYP 13 robotics and intelligent transportation challenge, awarded third place. The challenge explores how autonomous delivery vehicles can coordinate in a changing city without a central controller.

The challenge

Coordinate a heterogeneous fleet of delivery vehicles in a simulated urban environment. The brief brings together autonomous vehicles, ground robots and drones, with a focus on decentralized logistics and realistic communication limits.

Our simulation toolkit

We used ROS 2 with Gazebo for the robotics simulation and LiDAR for environmental sensing. This toolkit supported our work on autonomous mobility within the challenge’s urban simulation setting.

Delivery plans that adapt

The core problem is a Dynamic Vehicle Routing Problem (D-VRP): delivery requests arrive, change or get cancelled while vehicles are already moving. Task allocation must account for delivery deadlines, vehicle capacity and available energy.

Cooperation without a central controller

The brief calls for vehicles to allocate and adapt tasks through decentralized coordination. Peer-to-peer strategies, swarm intelligence and multi-agent optimization are possible approaches to balancing decisions across the fleet.

Communication is part of the problem

Vehicles exchange information within a limited range and at synchronized intervals inspired by 5G V2V connectivity. Routing and cooperation therefore need to account for when other vehicles can be reached, alongside delivery efficiency and energy consumption.

Third place at TSYP 13

Our IEEE ENET’Com Student Branch team received third place in the joint RAS and VTS challenge. The award photograph above documents this team result.
