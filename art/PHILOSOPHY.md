# Attitude Fields

A measured field acquires character through a small change in orientation. The work begins with evenly distributed bands on a spherical domain, then turns them through a coupled coordinate system. Its conceptual seed is the dependence of solar exposure on orientation: familiar to the CubeSat problem, expressed as an abstract study rather than scientific telemetry.

Each band follows the same mathematical rule, but seeded phase and coherent noise disturb its radius. This controlled variation makes the surface feel manufactured and unsettled at once. Curves are densely sampled; the result is a carefully resolved structure rather than a handful of decorative orbits. The visual interest is in the transition between adjacent strands.

Depth determines both brightness and weight. Far strands recede into a blue-black field; near strands carry cobalt, ice and a restrained warm reflection. The palette suggests a change in exposure without assigning a fictitious temperature scale. Fine points along selected strands reveal the sampled nature of the field.

Composition remains quiet: one asymmetric object, ample surrounding space, no labels pretending to be measurements. The viewer allows the seed, band density, twist, sampling resolution and tilt to change. Every seed is reproducible, every control changes the construction, and the image is rendered only on demand. The portfolio uses a static export to keep its reading experience fast and motion-free.
